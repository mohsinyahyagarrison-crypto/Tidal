How it works

  1. Pick a length

    Choose 25 or 50 minutes. Not sure? Start with 25.

  2. Focus until the water is full

    Work on one thing. The vessel fills slowly, so you can glance at it instead of watching a clock.

  3. Take a break that counts

    When it's full, Tidal suggests a five-minute break. Stand up, stretch, drink some water, then start again.

Built to stay out of your way

A timer should help you concentrate, not give you one more thing to manage.

  • One tap to start

    There are no setup screens between you and your first session.

  • Progress you can read at a glance

    The water level shows how far along you are. The exact time is always on screen if you want it.

  • Breaks without the nagging

    You get one quiet reminder at the end of a session. Tidal doesn't send streak warnings or guilt-trip notifications.

  • Made to be accessible

    Every control works with a keyboard and a screen reader. Text scales with your device settings, and animation stops if you've turned motion off.

Questions

Is Tidal really free?

Yes. The free plan has everything you need for daily studying. Plus adds custom session lengths, progress history and sync between devices.

Why a water level instead of a countdown?

A countdown makes you watch the clock. A filling vessel shows progress at a glance, so you can look away and look back. The numbers are still there whenever you want them.

Can I use Tidal with a keyboard or screen reader?

Yes. Every control can be reached and used with a keyboard, the timer announces when a session starts, pauses and ends, and the animation stops if your device is set to reduce motion.

What if I need a different session length?

The free plan covers 25 and 50 minutes. With Plus you can set any length from 5 to 120 minutes.

Design notes
  • Visibility of system status. The water level, the digits and a one-line status message change together. While a session runs, the browser tab shows the time left.
  • Error prevention and recovery. Session length is locked while a timer is running or paused, and Reset is always available. The email field explains what is wrong and how to fix it.
  • Consistency. One segmented control is used for session length and billing period. The waitlist action has the same name and style everywhere.
  • Recognition over recall. Every control has a text label. Nothing relies on an icon alone.
  • Accessibility. Atkinson Hyperlegible body text, text contrast of at least 4.5:1, touch targets of at least 44px, visible focus outlines, full keyboard support and reduced-motion support.
